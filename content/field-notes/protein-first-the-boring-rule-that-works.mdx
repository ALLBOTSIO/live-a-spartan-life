Most men do not need a diet. They need one rule they will still be following in March.

Here it is: **build every meal around a protein source before anything else joins the plate.**

That is the whole thing. It is unimpressive, it does not require an app, and it quietly solves several problems at once.

## Why it works when rules usually do not

**It is additive, not restrictive.** You are not banning anything. Elimination diets fail because they run on willpower and a grocery list of things you cannot have. This runs on one decision made at the start of a meal.

**It changes what is left.** A plate anchored to meat, fish, eggs, or dairy has less room for the stuff you were going to regret. You did not have to fight it — you crowded it out.

**It handles the afternoon.** Protein-forward meals hold you longer. The 3 p.m. collapse most men treat as a caffeine problem is often a breakfast problem.

**It supports the training.** You cannot build what you do not supply. If you are lifting twice a week and eating like a man who is not, you are paying for training you will not receive.

## The rough target

Somewhere around 0.7 to 1 gram per pound of target bodyweight per day is the range most active men land in. If you are 200 pounds and aiming there, that is roughly 140–200 grams.

Do not weigh anything. Count palms instead — a palm-sized portion of a protein source at each meal, twice at the largest one. If you hit that at three meals you are close enough, and "close enough, every day" beats "exact, for eleven days."

## What it looks like

- **Morning:** eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, or last night's meat. Not a pastry, not a bowl of cereal marketed as a health food.
- **Midday:** the protein first, then the vegetables, then whatever carbohydrate is around.
- **Evening:** the easiest meal to get right and the one most men do get right. Keep it.
- **The gap:** if you are hungry between meals, the last meal was short on protein. Adjust upstream rather than reaching for a snack designed to be eaten quickly.

## The two rules that ride along

**Water before caffeine.** Most men wake up mildly dehydrated and immediately reach for a diuretic. Sixteen ounces of water first. Then coffee, if you still want it — and you will want less of it.

**Whole food by default.** If a food needs a marketing claim to sound healthy, it is not the default. Exceptions are fine. Keep them honest and occasional rather than pretending they are not happening.

## Run it for seven days

Change nothing else. No macros app, no eliminations, no supplements.

Notice two things: what happens to your afternoon, and how often you were hungry for reasons that had nothing to do with hunger.

Boring rules kept for years beat perfect rules kept for nine days. That is the entire fuel pillar in one sentence.
